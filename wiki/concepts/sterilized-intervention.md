# Sterilized intervention

## Definition

Sterilized intervention is the purchase of foreign currency by the central bank, offset so that the domestic money stock does not rise. In the 1990–91 episode the offset was typically a sale of government bonds.

## Evidence

- Chile in 1990–91 and Colombia in 1991 are cited as leading examples. Sterilization can raise domestic interest rates and the fiscal burden while keeping the interest differential that attracts further inflows. Rodriguez (1991), as cited here, puts the fiscal burden of sterilized intervention in Colombia during 1991 at about 0.5 percent of GDP. Source: [Calvo, Leiderman, and Reinhart, capital inflows (1993)](../sources/1993-03-01-calvo-capital-inflows.md).
- The authors find it hard to make a strong case for sterilized intervention when fiscal positions are still being tightened. They instead favor a tax on short-term capital imports, more exchange-rate flexibility, and higher marginal reserve requirements on short-term deposits. Source: [Calvo, Leiderman, and Reinhart, capital inflows (1993)](../sources/1993-03-01-calvo-capital-inflows.md).
