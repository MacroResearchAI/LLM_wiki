# Latin American capital inflows

## Thesis

The 1990–91 surge in capital inflows to Latin America, and the accompanying [real exchange rate](../concepts/real-exchange-rate.md) appreciation, is largely an external shock from US interest rates, the US recession, and US capital-market conditions. Domestic reforms were necessary for the reentry, but they do not explain why inflows moved together across countries. Policy can limit the damage from a reversal; it is unlikely to undo the appreciation itself.

## Supporting evidence

- Inflows rose from about $8 billion a year in the second half of the 1980s to $24 billion in 1990 and $40 billion in 1991. About 45 percent of the 1991 total went to Mexico. Source: [Calvo, Leiderman, and Reinhart, capital inflows (1993)](../sources/1993-03-01-calvo-capital-inflows.md).
- 1990 and 1991 were the first net private capital outflows from the United States after eight years of net inflows. Terms-of-trade declines of 1.2 percent in 1990 and 5.2 percent in 1991 are too small to explain the surge. Source: [Calvo, Leiderman, and Reinhart, capital inflows (1993)](../sources/1993-03-01-calvo-capital-inflows.md).
- For most of ten countries over January 1988–November 1991, foreign factors account for about half of monthly real-exchange-rate forecast-error variance, and they are statistically related to reserves and the real exchange rate in eight of the ten. Source: [Calvo, Leiderman, and Reinhart, capital inflows (1993)](../sources/1993-03-01-calvo-capital-inflows.md).
- [Sterilized intervention](../concepts/sterilized-intervention.md) was the most common response. The authors prefer a tax on short-term capital imports, more exchange-rate flexibility, and higher marginal reserve requirements on short-term deposits. Source: [Calvo, Leiderman, and Reinhart, capital inflows (1993)](../sources/1993-03-01-calvo-capital-inflows.md).

## Contradicting evidence

- The same paper says domestic reforms, including external-debt restructuring, were a necessary ingredient. Without them, the scale of the inflows would have been hard to attract. Source: [Calvo, Leiderman, and Reinhart, capital inflows (1993)](../sources/1993-03-01-calvo-capital-inflows.md).

## Open questions

- How much of the inflow was medium-term investment, and how much was short-term "hot money"?
- How large an outflow would follow if US rates rose or US capital outflows reversed?
